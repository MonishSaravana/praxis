# Deck v2.11: changes since v2.10

13 core slides, the appendix divider, and 12 appendix slides.

## New order (the story)

Problem → product → how it works → built → measured → why now → competitors → why nobody owns the niche → market +
pricing → savings + go-to-market → long game → ask.

The logic: show the pain and what we built, prove it works, then say why now, who else is out there, how big it is,
how we make money, where it goes, and what the money buys.

## Changes

- **Why now:** new title, "Smart glasses took off, and AI keeps getting cheaper". The right chart is now a
  market-wide trend: a16z's 1,000× drop in three years, from $60 to $0.06 per million tokens at GPT-3-level
  capability. Our own cost ($1.08 vs $5.10 an hour) is off this slide; it stays on the measured slide as ours.
- **Why nobody owns this niche:** "$99 → $25" is replaced by "$0": Meta AI and Gemini Live already give camera help
  away free to consumers.
- **Market + pricing:** a "How we charge" panel: paid pilot → $200 per trainee a month (about six months) →
  one-time setup per procedure → $50 per tech a month for records (next).
- **No small subtext:** source lines, the email, the pilot-metric line and the outreach line are off the slides.
  Each slide's sources are in its speaker notes.
- **Appendix divider:** "INFO SLIDES / Appendix", built from the same list that orders the appendix, so the two
  can't drift apart.
- **New A2, "What we train in-house, and why a big AI lab won't build this":** for the technical question in Q&A.
