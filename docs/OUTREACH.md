## The email

**Subject:** Harvard student project, quick question

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a Harvard student working on a project about how maintenance teams train new techs. I'm exploring a tool that
> watches through a smart-glasses camera and talks a new tech through a job step by step, so they need less
> shadowing.
>
> Could I ask you a few quick questions? Even a one-line answer would help a ton:
>
> 1. How do you train new techs right now, and how long until they can work on their own?
> 2. Roughly what does it cost to train one (senior-tech time, mistakes, etc.)?
> 3. Would you be up for trying something like this on one job? Totally fine if not!
>
> Thanks so much,
> Monish

**Why these three questions:**
- Questions 1 and 2 are the pain and what it costs.
- Question 3 is whether anyone actually wants our fix.
- Save price and details for anyone who writes back.

**Follow-up** (Monday evening, only if there's no reply):
> "Just bumping this in case it got buried. Even a quick answer to #1 would really help!"

## A faster channel (optional)

Forums where technicians and managers hang out answer on weekends, sometimes within hours: for example r/HVAC and
an industrial-maintenance subreddit.
- Check each community's rules on surveys and self-promotion first.
- Post question 1, framed as "how does your shop train new techs?" Skip the tool question on forums.
- You post it yourself.
- Forum answers are mostly from technicians, not buyers. Quote them as "technicians told me", not as buyer demand.

## How to use the answers on Tuesday (keep it honest)

- **Say what happened:**
  - "I emailed 20 maintenance managers this weekend. Three replied."
  - "One maintenance supervisor at a Massachusetts plant said a new tech takes about X weeks of shadowing."
- **Don't name people or companies** without their permission. Don't call a reply a "customer", a "pilot" or an
  "LOI".
- **If nobody replies:** "I've reached out to N maintenance managers. The first use of the grant is ten of these
  conversations, and the first pilot comes out of them."
- **If an answer contradicts the plan, say so.** For example, if they say shadowing is fine. It's the strongest
  signal that you're running a real experiment.
