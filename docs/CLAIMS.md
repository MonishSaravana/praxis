# What I can say in the interview (and what I can't)

Every sentence below comes from a file under `results/`, linked after it. This file is generated by `prototype/praxis/write_results.py` from `results/metrics.json`. Numbers are rounded for speech: percentages to whole numbers, everything else as shown. Re-generate it after any re-run.

## Safe to say

- "We evaluated on 48 held-out first-person clips, about 235 minutes from the public CaptainCook4D dataset. They cover 8 procedures, with 302 annotated steps and 170 annotated mistakes. All labels are the dataset's; none are ours." (`results/data/eval_manifest_summary.json`)
- "On 48 held-out clips, with the task known, our stateful engine tracked the current step correctly 54% of the time. Calling the same frontier model every 5 seconds without state got 48%." (`results/runs/fused_oracle.json`)
- "It costs about 5x less to run: $0.018 vs $0.085 per minute of video, because most checks are fast typed questions and the frontier model is only called to escalate." (`results/runs/fused_oracle.json`)
- "On mistakes, both systems are weak and we're honest about it. Strictly scored, Praxis's alerts named the actual annotated error 4 times out of 17; the stateless baseline did 2 out of 3. So Praxis catches more real mistakes but is less precise, and both catch only a small share of the 170 annotated errors." (`results/l6/mistake_semantic_fused_oracle.json`)
- "The mistakes Praxis does catch tend to need task state: 4 of the 4 annotated errors its alerts correctly named are in steps the dataset marks as done out of order, the most common error type in the data (96 of 170). You can only see that kind of error if you know where the person is in the task, so better step tracking should mean more catches." (`results/l6/mistake_semantic_fused_oracle.json`)
- "Counting every alert that didn't name an annotated error as a false alarm, which is strict because some may be real but unannotated, Praxis raised about one every 18 minutes of video." (`results/l6/mistake_semantic_fused_oracle.json`)
- "Only 14% of frames leave the local layers, and 88% of those model calls are answered by the fast tier (Jev, ~0.2 s); the rest escalate to the frontier model." (`results/metrics.json: call_mix_fused_oracle (counted from the run logs)`)
- "With no setup at all, it recognized which recipe was being made on 73% of held-out clips, with a median of 40 seconds." (`results/runs/fused.json`)
- "With the task given, fusing local evidence with the verifier gives 54% step accuracy, vs 41% from local evidence alone." (`results/runs/fused_oracle.json`)
- "On the same 469 labeled checks, Jev answered in 151 ms (median) at AUROC 0.70. Claude Opus answered in 3.8 s at AUROC 0.84. So we use Jev as the fast tier and escalate ambiguous cases to Opus." (`results/l5/compare_jev_vs_claude_fast.json`)
- "Calibration on training data cut Jev's expected calibration error from 0.22 to 0.02 on held-out checks." (`results/l5/calibration.json`)
- "We distilled the frontier verifier into a local model that agrees with it on 69% of held-out checks. It answers in 0.03 ms on a laptop CPU and costs nothing per check. Sending it only the checks it's confident about keeps teacher-level accuracy while removing 34% of frontier calls." (`results/l7/distill.json`)
- "When we degraded the view (blur, occlude, crop away), Praxis asked for a better view on 77-98% of checks and was never confidently wrong. The stateless baseline has to answer, and it named the wrong step with at least 50% confidence on 12-73% of them (2-17% at 70% confidence)." (`results/l5/visual_uncertainty.json`)
- "Inside the full engine, Jev as the fast tier gave 54% step accuracy at $0.018 per video-minute; Claude Haiku in the same slot gave 47% at $0.007." (`results/runs/fused_claude_fast.json`)
- "Escalating to the frontier model mostly cuts false alarms: with it, 4 of 17 mistake alerts named a real annotated error; without it, 3 of 24 (0.055 vs 0.089 strict false alarms per minute). Step accuracy barely changes (54% with it, 53% without). It costs $0.018 vs $0.001 per video-minute." (`results/runs/fused_no_escalation.json`)
- "Swapping the local student in for routine completion checks gives 52% step accuracy (vs 54%) at $0.008 per video-minute (vs $0.018). It's a one-line config change." (`results/runs/fused_distilled.json`)
- "We tested zoomed-in crops from point tracking (TAPNext++) on 120 fine-grained checks. They barely changed the verifier: accuracy 79% without vs 79% with, AUROC 0.85 vs 0.86, and tracking took about 9 s per crop on a laptop. So crops are off by default." (`results/l5/crops_vs_full.json`)
- "The whole engine runs live in real time on a laptop. We fed a recorded clip through the live loop at real-time speed: it recognized the recipe in 22 seconds and gave step-by-step instructions as the person worked, with the local layers taking about 50 ms per frame." (`results/live/live_replay_async.json`)
- "Model calls run in the background, so the live loop never waits for the network. On the same recording, the worst tick lag went from 4.4 s with calls inline to 0.8 s, and dropped frames from 28 to 3. While the slowest request (a Jev completion check escalated to Opus, 4.4 s) was out, the loop ran 4 more ticks." (`results/live/live_replay_async.json`, `results/live/live_replay_10_18_0.json`)
- "For recipes it had never seen, Praxis didn't stay silent. When the person said what they were making, it drafted a step-by-step plan in about 16 seconds (on 7 of 8 recordings). The drafts covered 64% of the recipe's steps in the dataset's own task graph on average, and Praxis tracked the person's step with them 36% of the time, with no training video." (`results/open_set/open_set.json`)
- "From video alone after 30 seconds, it named the recipe on 0 of 7 recordings (2 more partly), so for unknown tasks the product should ask what you're making rather than guess." (`results/open_set/open_set.json`)

## Do NOT claim (not measured or not true yet)

- That the models get 'exponentially' better with data. The learning curves show steady gains per doubling of training data. Also don't say the full engine got better from them: the engine's own step models used 3 recordings per procedure, and it hasn't been re-run with more (results/scaling/).
- That the step-change detector improves with more data. Its learning-curve run was interrupted, and the runs that finished were noisy with no clear trend from 6 to 24 recordings (results/scaling/learning_curve.json, detector_partial).
- Praxis runs on smart glasses. It has not been tested on glasses; the glasses adapter is a documented stub (docs/GLASSES.md). Say instead: 'it runs live on a video stream in real time; glasses are one more input adapter.'
- Any result on toy-car or drone assembly. The IndustReal assembly clips have no ground truth here (labels were unreachable this weekend); they are qualitative demos only.
- Any user study, customer, pilot, or 'helps people learn faster'. Adaptive detail is an association on public data, and on CaptainCook4D it was NOT associated with annotated errors (see RESULTS.md).
- Real-time point tracking. TAPNext++ ran offline for the crop experiment; it was too slow on this Mac for live use.
- That Praxis can coach any task out of the box. Open-set drafts were tested on 4 unseen recipes (8 recordings) in one domain; drafts have no mistake library, and the video-only draft often misnamed the task (results/open_set/). It also sometimes mistakes an unseen task for a known one (a mug cake was announced as the library's mug pizza).
- That live mode was tested with a webcam. It was tested on a recorded clip replayed in real time (no camera was available to this build). Also don't say feedback is instant: the loop no longer waits for model calls, but an answer still takes as long as the model. Completion checks took effect up to 4.8 s after their frames in the async run (results/live/live_replay_async.json).
- That the concept video is model output. It stays labeled 'Concept Prototype'.
- The application's '48 videos / 8 categories / 192 labeled action states' as if they were this build's set. This build's eval set is 48 clips / 8 procedure types / 302 annotated step segments / 170 timed errors (CaptainCook4D). Quote these instead.
- Mistake precision from time-window matching (it is optimistic). Use the strict, judge-verified numbers.
- That the distilled student replaces the frontier model. It handles routine completion checks; localization stays with the fast verifier (see RESULTS: fused_distilled vs fused_distilled_alone).
